# Code of conduct

> **Draft.** Written for the owner of this repository to accept, change
> or replace. It is not in force until this note is removed.

## What this is for

People come here to make a testing tool better and to get help using it.
This page says how we treat each other while doing that. It applies to
the issues, pull requests, discussions and reviews of this repository,
and to anyone acting for the project elsewhere.

## What we ask

- Talk about the code, the report or the idea. Not about the person.
- Assume the other person means well and may know something you do not.
  Ask before you conclude.
- Say plainly when you disagree, and give the reason. A failing seed, a
  measurement or a reference settles more than insistence does.
- Accept a review as help. Give one the same way: say what is wrong, why,
  and what would fix it.
- Leave room for people who are new, who write in a second language, or
  who work differently from you.
- When you were wrong, say so and go on. Everyone here has been.

## What is not accepted

- Insults, mockery or contempt, aimed at a person or at a group.
- Remarks or jokes that demean people for who they are: their origin,
  sex, gender, orientation, age, body, health, religion or belief.
- Sexual remarks or advances.
- Threats, or encouraging harm to anyone.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Continuing to contact or argue with someone who has asked you to stop.
- Deliberately derailing a discussion, or wearing people down until they
  give in.

## Reporting a problem

If something here has crossed these lines, toward you or someone else,
report it privately. Do not open a public issue about it. Use the same
private route as for vulnerabilities, described in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md): the "Report a vulnerability" button on this
repository's Security tab. Start the title with "Conduct" so it is not
read as a security report. Say what happened, where (a link helps), and
when.

Reports are read by the maintainers of the repository. They are kept
private, and the person who reports is not named to the person reported
without their agreement. A maintainer who is the subject of a report
takes no part in handling it.

## What happens then

The maintainers look at what was said and its context, and choose the
mildest response that stops the problem:

1. A private word, saying what was wrong.
2. A public correction, or hiding or removing the comment.
3. A temporary block from the repository.
4. A permanent block.

Serious or repeated cases skip steps. The person concerned is told what
was decided and why.

## Scope of this page

This is about conduct, not about technical decisions. A pull request can
be declined, and a design argued against, firmly and within every line
above.
